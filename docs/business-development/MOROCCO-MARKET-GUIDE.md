# Morocco market guide

## Why Morocco is a strong first market

Morocco combines sustained water pressure with a large, visible programme of institutional and infrastructure change.

The **Programme National d’Approvisionnement en Eau Potable et d’Irrigation 2020–2027** mobilised an announced MAD 115.4 billion for drinking-water security, supply diversification, demand management, rural service and treated-wastewater reuse. The programme and its financing are described by the [Moroccan Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://www.finances.gov.ma/fr/Pages/detail-actualite.aspx?fiche=4680).

At distribution level, Morocco is consolidating water, electricity and sanitation services into twelve regional multiservice companies. In June 2025, the government reported a combined **MAD 253 billion investment programme** for the Sociétés Régionales Multiservices (SRMs) and described the transfer of assets, contracts, personnel and operating records from predecessor organisations. The [official Maroc.ma summary](https://www.maroc.ma/fr/actualites/les-srm-mettent-en-place-un-programme-dinvestissement-de-253-milliards-de-dh) makes asset transition, service continuity and record quality central market themes.

Non-conventional water is expanding rapidly. [OCP Green Water](https://www.ocpgroup.ma/en/who-we-are/our-strategic-entities-and-partners) reports that it covered all OCP Group industrial water needs from non-conventional sources in 2025. Its [current water programme](https://www.ocpgroup.ma/en/taxonomy/term/26) covers desalination, drinking-water supply and transfer infrastructure at national scale.

Cybersecurity governance also affects the market. Under Morocco’s [Law 05-20 framework](https://www.dgssi.gov.ma/fr/si-siiv), sensitive information systems belonging to infrastructures of vital importance are classified, declared and subject to security homologation before operation. Atlas is not a security-homologation or certification service; its relevant contribution is a bounded, evidence-linked understanding of the assets and records being reviewed.

## What motivates Moroccan prospects

### Continuity of water service

Operational continuity is the strongest motivation. Water operators and industrial water producers are judged on reliable production, pumping, treatment, distribution and sanitation. Atlas should therefore be connected to fewer uncertainties during maintenance, commissioning and incident preparation rather than to abstract cybersecurity maturity.

### Regional consolidation and inherited assets

The SRM transition brings together assets and records from ONEE branches, municipal régies and delegated operators. Public policy explicitly refers to asset, contract and personnel transfer. A relevant message is:

> Establish a reviewed baseline for one area before inherited records become accepted assumptions.

### Telemanagement and automation renewal

ONEE publishes technical clauses for [telemanagement and automation systems](https://www.onep.org.ma/Espace-Achats-ONEE-BO/Cahiers-Clauses-Techniques_dispo-Generales/Cahiers-Clauses-Techniques_dispo-Generales.htm). Regional operators also publish current work in this area; for example, the [SRM Fès–Meknès procurement page](https://www.radeef.ma/appel-offres/avis) includes telemanagement-related notices. Prospects already use SCADA, PLC, RTU, instrumentation and remote-station terminology, so Atlas should be presented as a complementary evidence and review tool.

### GIS and asset-data reconciliation

Regional operators are modernising field records, GIS and customer systems. The practical question is not simply whether an inventory exists, but which record should be accepted when the GIS, project file, maintenance list, field observation and network evidence disagree.

### Commissioning and EPC handover

Desalination, transfer pipelines, treatment facilities and pumping infrastructure involve EPCs, operators, integrators and multiple acceptance packages. Atlas is most credible when offered for one bounded train, station, package or handover decision.

### Cybersecurity evidence without production disruption

Water organisations are cautious about any tool that could interfere with operations. Atlas can begin with approved documents or imported PCAP/PCAPNG evidence and uses a passive-first workflow. This makes the initial conversation about evidence quality and operational decisions rather than intrusive testing.

### Cost control and local autonomy

The repository is public and the customer may build an equivalent device. This is a commercial advantage: the customer is not locked into proprietary hardware or a mandatory subscription. Paid value comes from a prepared appliance, installation, configuration and specialist OT networking or security services.

## Market segments

### Public water and multiservice operators

The primary organisations are [ONEE – Branche Eau](https://www.onep.org.ma/) and the twelve SRMs. They operate geographically distributed estates and procure pumping, treatment, telemanagement, instrumentation, GIS and sanitation work.

Strong entry cases include:

- a new or upgraded telemanagement package;
- a pumping station or treatment facility entering service;
- an inherited asset register after operator transition;
- a maintenance baseline for a remote-station group;
- a discrepancy between field evidence and GIS or as-built records.

### Industrial and non-conventional water operators

[OCP Green Water](https://www.linkedin.com/company/ocpgreenwater) is the leading example. Relevant motivations are commissioning quality, operational continuity, EPC handover and long-term asset governance.

### Engineering, EPC and asset-management partners

Engineering organisations can use Atlas as an additional handover or field-verification deliverable. Relevant public examples include [JESA](https://www.linkedin.com/company/jesa-s-a), [NOVEC](https://www.linkedin.com/company/novec-sa/), [Waterplus Morocco](https://www.linkedin.com/company/waterplus-maroc) and [Aquatech Services des Eaux Maroc](https://www.linkedin.com/company/aquatech-services-des-eaux-maroc/).

### Automation and telemanagement integrators

Local SCADA, automation and telemanagement firms can be referral partners, installation partners or users during acceptance work. Atlas complements their engineering tools by producing a portable, reviewable evidence package for the operator.

## Positioning

### Recommended description

> Atlas OT Scout is a portable offline appliance that helps water and industrial teams establish an evidence-linked baseline for one bounded OT area. It compares expected records with approved observations, highlights conflicts and unresolved identities, and supports commissioning, maintenance, inventory reconciliation and security review.

### Lead with

- operational continuity;
- project acceptance and handover;
- asset and record reconciliation;
- a free, reversible device evaluation;
- local control and open implementation;
- optional OT networking and security consulting.

### Keep the boundary clear

Atlas is not a broad network scanner, a replacement for operational platforms, a certification audit or a continuous-monitoring system. The repository currently documents an executable research prototype and candidate appliance hardware; marketing material must not imply completed production qualification.

## Language and communication style

French is the default language for technical and procurement communication. Arabic is important for institutional and public-service communication. English is suitable for OCP Group, international engineering organisations and technology partners.

Messages should be concrete and project-based. A useful opening refers to a public investment, commissioning event, telemanagement project or asset-transfer issue, then asks how the organisation currently verifies the accepted asset state.

## Initial market priorities

1. Demonstrate a specific water-sector decision outcome rather than generic cybersecurity capability.
2. Use a bounded synthetic example to show expected, corroborated, conflicting and unresolved records.
3. Make the free 30-day evaluation the primary call to action.
4. Select organisations using current project, procurement and commissioning signals.
5. Develop relationships with engineering, automation and water-operations partners.
6. Publish practical French-language content on handover evidence, telemanagement baselines and offline OT assessment.
