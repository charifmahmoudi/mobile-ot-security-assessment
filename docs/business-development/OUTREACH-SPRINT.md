## Discovery call structure

### Opening — 3 minutes

- cite the public signal that prompted the discussion;
- state the problem hypothesis;
- confirm that the purpose is validation, not a production-access request;
- ask permission to take factual notes.

### Evidence — 20 minutes

Obtain explicit answers to:

1. What exact decision is difficult?
2. What is the current method and who owns it?
3. What fails, takes too long or creates disputes?
4. What consequence follows if the issue remains unresolved?
5. What dated event creates urgency?
6. Which inventory, GIS, as-built package or commissioning record is authoritative?
7. Who reviews and accepts technical evidence?
8. Who owns budget and procurement?
9. What existing tool, contractor or process addresses the problem?
10. Which bounded artifact, lab or process area could be reviewed safely?
11. What would cause Atlas to be rejected?
12. Which commercial structure would be acceptable?

### Close — 7 minutes

Read back:

> Ce que j’ai compris est que [décision] est difficile parce que [problème], que cela compte avant [événement/date], et que [fonctions] doivent valider le résultat.

Then obtain exactly one next step:

- referral to the functional owner;
- review of the representative evidence package;
- second meeting with the technical evaluator;
- pilot-boundary workshop;
- explicit decision that the problem is not relevant.

Do not close with an unspecified promise to send information.
