# Morocco prospect intelligence directory

_Reviewed: 2 September 2026._

This page owns **account- and person-level public sales intelligence** for Atlas OT Scout in Morocco. A company is not sufficiently researched because an executive name is known. The useful unit is the **buying and evaluation group**: the person who owns the technical or operational problem, the person who can evaluate Atlas, the sponsor who can authorize a bounded evaluation, and the practitioners or governance contacts who can move the work forward.

The [commercial diligence summary](DILIGENCE-SUMMARY.md) owns cross-market evidence and conclusions. The [market guide](MOROCCO-MARKET-GUIDE.md) owns segmentation and entry situations. The [marketing playbook](MARKETING-PLAYBOOK.md) owns message construction. This directory answers: **which organization, which people, why each person matters, why now, and how strong is the evidence?**

## How to read the people maps

### Stakeholder classes

| Class | Meaning | Typical Atlas role |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 — technical evaluator / problem owner** | Owns relevant engineering, operations, automation, asset-data or security work | Best first contact for determining whether Atlas solves a real problem |
| **T2 — sponsor / governance** | Executive, director or business owner who can authorize attention, resources or an evaluation | Sponsor once a concrete technical use case exists |
| **T3 — practitioner / connector** | Relevant practitioner with useful field context but unclear purchasing authority | Discovery, technical feedback or route into the correct team |

### Evidence grades

- **V1:** current first-party organization source, official procurement/program material, or direct organization publication.
- **V2:** current public professional profile with employer/role, or strong role-specific public professional activity.
- **V3:** current secondary directory, copied organization document or third-party profile. Useful for research, but it must not be the principal evidence supporting an `A` account.

Do not infer personal email addresses, personal phone numbers or private contact details. A public professional profile is a valid route. A direct business address is retained only when the organization itself publishes it.

### Account readiness

- **A — outreach-ready:** current account trigger; at least one strong T1; a second current role-specific stakeholder who gives a credible multi-threaded route; sponsor/governance path; and the core contact path is supported predominantly by V1/V2 evidence.
- **B+ — near-ready:** at least one strong current T1 and a plausible sponsor/second thread exist, but one important technical layer or current title still needs verification.
- **B — partially mapped:** the account is relevant but does not yet have a dependable T1-led contact path.
- **C — research queue:** the organization is relevant but current person-level intelligence is insufficient.

An executive plus one loosely related employee is **not** enough for `A`.

## People-research rules

1. **Relevance beats seniority.** An automation, asset-management, project or operations engineer may be a better first prospect than a Director General.
2. **Current role beats historical fit.** Historical technical work can establish domain expertise, but a person must still have a current connection to the target account.
3. Prefer first-party sources, official procurement/program material, current organization posts and direct public professional profiles.
4. Record role, stakeholder class, evidence grade and verification date.
5. A public employee list is only a discovery source; do not promote a name without role/activity evidence.
6. V3 can generate leads but should not be the foundation of an `A` account.
7. Do not infer personal email patterns, personal phone numbers or private contact details.
8. Keep professional activity only when it creates a legitimate hook: project delivery, commissioning, automation, OT/security, asset management, maintenance, GIS/CMMS, water operations, audit methodology or infrastructure transition.
9. Separate technical usefulness from buying authority.
10. Recheck roles immediately before outreach; SRM structures are changing quickly.
11. A public project is a reason to investigate, not evidence that Atlas is needed.
12. When evidence contradicts an earlier entry, correct the directory rather than preserving a stale name for apparent completeness.
