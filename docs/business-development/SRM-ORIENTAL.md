## Verified public evidence

- **Priority programme:** Business reporting states that the SRM commissioned a priority programme to restructure water and sanitation networks.
- **GIS inventory:** A 2026 public procurement notice covers field recognition, restitution and GIS entry for water, sanitation and electricity assets across the region.
- **Current works:** 2026 notices include Oujda water extensions, network rehabilitation and treated-wastewater distribution for green-space irrigation.

## Current public signals

- **Direct inventory signal:** The GIS field-recognition procurement is unusually close to Atlas's inventory-reconciliation proposition.
- **Reuse infrastructure:** Treated-wastewater transfer and distribution create cross-domain pumping, storage and control-evidence needs.
- **Regional heterogeneity:** The work spans Oujda-Angad, Nador, Driouch, Jerada, Berkane, Taourirt, Guercif and Figuig.

## Atlas entry wedge

Lead with a project-side evidence companion for the GIS recognition programme: verify one water or sanitation zone, preserve provenance, flag conflicts and deliver a review queue rather than replacing the GIS.

Keep the first engagement to one operator, one process area and one decision. Passive or imported evidence is the default; any packet-producing action requires exact written authorization.

## Buying group and qualification

**Roles to identify:** Investment and planning directorate; water operations; sanitation and reuse programme; GIS and asset-data team; provincial operations; project acceptance; information systems and cybersecurity.

**Questions:** Who owns the GIS data model and acceptance criteria? Which field-recognition lot has the highest unresolved identity risk? Can Atlas export an evidence delta compatible with the GIS workflow? Which reuse project has a bounded commissioning segment?

**Risks:** A tender mirror must be rechecked against the official procurement portal before outreach. GIS work does not prove OT-inventory quality or a specific protocol estate. Figuig and other local contexts require careful stakeholder and service-continuity planning.

Public sources do not prove installed vendors, protocols, topology, vulnerabilities, budget availability or permission to test.
