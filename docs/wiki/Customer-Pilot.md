# Customer Pilot

The Atlas pilot is designed to answer one operationally useful question for one bounded OT control area.

## Recommended objective

> **Determine whether the installed control equipment supports the asset inventory currently accepted by the customer, and identify the discrepancies that require follow-up.**

A pilot should validate customer value, not merely demonstrate that the software runs.

## Good pilot scope

A suitable first pilot has:

- one water or wastewater process area;
- a manageable expected asset baseline;
- an identified operational owner;
- approved evidence access;
- a clear assessment question;
- a defined review and handoff audience.

## Customer inputs

The customer provides, where available:

- expected asset inventory;
- relevant drawings or commissioning context;
- approved PCAP/PCAPNG or SPAN/TAP access;
- operational and security authorization;
- known exclusions and stop conditions;
- a reviewer or stakeholder able to validate the result.

## Atlas assessment

The practitioner:

1. defines and authorizes the case;
2. loads the expected baseline;
3. imports or collects bounded evidence;
4. reconciles expected and observed state;
5. investigates selected identity gaps where explicitly authorized;
6. reviews material findings and limitations;
7. finalizes the assessment package.

## Customer deliverables

The pilot should return:

- reconciled asset baseline;
- confirmed, probable and unresolved matches;
- material identity conflicts;
- unexpected observations;
- expected records not observed in the available evidence;
- evidence-backed findings;
- visibility and method limitations;
- record of bounded active operations, if any;
- reviewed assessment output suitable for handoff.

## Success criteria

The pilot is successful when the customer can answer, more confidently than before:

- Which records in the current inventory are supported by evidence?
- Which records need correction or further investigation?
- Which observed assets are not represented in the accepted baseline?
- Which expected assets were not evidenced during the assessment?
- Which conclusions are strong, and which remain uncertain?

The pilot should also record whether Atlas reduced manual reconciliation effort, improved traceability, or surfaced discrepancies that the existing process did not make clear.

## What the pilot does not claim

A successful pilot does not by itself prove:

- complete network visibility;
- enterprise-wide asset coverage;
- vulnerability absence or presence from service observation alone;
- process safety or business impact without customer context;
- a generalized ROI beyond the measured pilot.

## Next decision

A pilot should end with one explicit outcome: **continue**, **modify and retest**, **investigate procurement**, or **stop**.
