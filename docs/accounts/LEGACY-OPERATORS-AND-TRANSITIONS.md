# Legacy operators and transition boundaries

_Research baseline: **1 September 2026**._

Morocco's SRM reform consolidates regional water, electricity and sanitation services, but public evidence shows that the transition is not uniform. This page prevents the commercial account map from treating historical operators as either universally current or universally obsolete.

## Transition map

| Operator | Publicly evidenced position | Account implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Lydec** | Veolia announced an agreement in July 2024 to sell its entire Lydec stake to SRM Casablanca–Settat. The SRM-CS official site says Lydec and the regional régies/ONEE activities were consolidated into the SRM. | Use Lydec records as predecessor evidence. The current commercial route is SRM-CS unless an exact contract or project document says otherwise. |
| **Redal** | SRM-RSK's official figures explicitly exclude the Redal delegated-management perimeter. Redal also appears as a procuring entity for a 2026 sanitation project. | Determine the exact commune and asset boundary before naming the buyer or authorization authority. |
| **Amendis** | Amendis continues to publish current operational indicators for Tanger and Tétouan, while SRM-TTA is awarding regional water works. | Confirm current operator, contract and transition status for every site. Treat Amendis as a live boundary question, not merely history. |

## Rules for account qualification

- Name the exact commune, facility, process area and asset owner.
- Identify the entity that operates the asset **today**, not only the regional brand.
- Identify who holds the authoritative inventory, maintenance contract and network-authorization power.
- Ask whether a transition, novation, asset transfer or data migration is scheduled during the proposed pilot.
- Keep predecessor and successor evidence separate until the customer validates the mapping.
- Never reuse a legacy operator's network diagrams, contacts or technical claims as current facts without permission and confirmation.

## Atlas opportunity in a transition

A transition can create a strong but sensitive use case:

1. import both predecessor and successor inventories;
2. preserve source and record dates;
3. collect passive evidence for one bounded area;
4. show duplicate, missing, conflicting and unverified identities;
5. require a named reviewer to accept or reject each proposed merge;
6. export an evidence-linked handover delta.

This is an asset-governance workflow, not a penetration test and not a claim that either operator's data is defective.
