# Cosumar account map

_Research baseline: 30 August 2026. Site-level technology and current management titles require confirmation before use._

## Group and industrial structure

Cosumar is Morocco's sole sugar operator and groups Cosumar SA, SUTA, Sucrafor, Surac and Sunabel. The public footprint covers five agricultural regions and approximately 80,000 growers. Company and industry material describe eight to nine industrial sites depending on reporting scope, including the Casablanca refinery and beet/cane processing facilities.

This is a seasonal, multi-site process-manufacturing account. Campaign readiness, water and energy efficiency, production continuity and the reconciliation of older and newer automation are more credible entry themes than a generic cybersecurity message.

### Reconstructed approval path

For one plant, a likely working group is site director/production → maintenance/automation → Industrial Director → IT & Digital Transformation → procurement/HSE. For group rollout, subsidiary/site management must join because campaign timing and installed equipment differ.

The reporting chain below the published directors is not official and must be confirmed.

## Industrial technology signals

The most specific public signals are:

- a professional history responsible for Yokogawa CS3000/CENTUM VP DCS development and maintenance across four Cosumar factories;
- a project profile describing communication between an Allen-Bradley PLC and Yokogawa DCS during Cosumar work;
- an OT-administration profile describing maintenance of industrial IT hardware including PLCs and workstations;
- a Moroccan integrator page describing Cosumar production-line automation, PID control for evaporators, centrifuge/dryer control and SCADA supervision;
- company sustainability material describing modernization of industrial facilities and ISO 50001 deployment across sites.

These sources support Yokogawa, Allen-Bradley, PLC/DCS, SCADA and industrial-workstation research priorities. They do not establish current versions, network architecture or deployment at all sites.

## What the company and others say publicly

Cosumar says it has invested heavily in industrial modernization, reduced water and energy intensity and introduced biomass and solar drying. SIAM 2026 coverage reports that the 2024/25 campaign produced more than two million tonnes of beet and 281,000 tonnes of white sugar despite drought, with large increases over the prior campaign.

The operational implication is a short, high-value campaign window: poor inventory or unexpected control-system dependencies can affect readiness. The product proposition should be “prepare and document the campaign safely,” not “your factory is insecure.”

## Opportunity designs

### Pre-campaign inventory reconciliation

During a scheduled shutdown at one processing or packaging area, import the maintenance inventory, collect passive evidence and resolve discrepancies before campaign startup.

### DCS/PLC documentation pack

An approved automation integrator records controller/workstation/network evidence during modification work and submits it to both Industrial Operations and IT/Digital review.

### Energy/utilities benchmark

Start with boilers, power distribution, water treatment or packaging utilities where modernization and ISO 50001 create an existing measurement/documentation context.

## Product implications

- Campaign/shutdown scope and expiration must be explicit.
- Process tags, controller identities and physical assets need separate but linked records.
- Passive Yokogawa/Allen-Bradley clues should carry source and confidence, not automatically become asset facts.
- French offline reports and CSV/JSON exports are required.
- A group dashboard is secondary to site-owned review and integration with existing CMMS/industrial systems.
- The product must preserve older-system ambiguity rather than force a modern taxonomy.
