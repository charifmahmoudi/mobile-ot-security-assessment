# Water-sector contact plans

These plans replace the former cross-industry prospect list. They cover only Moroccan drinking-water and wastewater organizations.

## Contact sequence

1. **Confirm the legal/operator boundary.** Name the province, site, process area and whether the asset belongs to ONEE, an SRM, a delegated operator or a contractor.
2. **Use an official route.** Prefer an organization contact form, procurement notice, tender clarification channel, event introduction or a public professional profile.
3. **Ask for redirection, not authority.** Request the person responsible for water automation/telemanagement, asset management, SCADA/OT security or project acceptance.
4. **Offer a small artifact exchange.** Ask for a redacted inventory template, representative topology or lab capture—not production access.
5. **Only then define a pilot.** One process area, written scope, passive first, exact stop conditions and named reviewers.

| SRM Tanger–Tétouan–Al Hoceïma | Current water/sanitation tender or provincial route | Network operations, leak-reduction, instrumentation, maintenance | Treating the full regional estate as one assessment scope |

## Suggested opening

> We are validating a passive-first Android field workflow for reconciling an authorized water or wastewater asset inventory. We are looking for the owner of one bounded treatment, pumping, reservoir, district-metering or sanitation area who could review a redacted evidence template or a lab demonstration. We are not requesting production access or proposing a broad network scan.

## Information to request

- exact operator and site owner;
- process area and operational window;
- existing inventory format and diagram owner;
- acceptable passive evidence source;
- incumbent integrator or maintenance contractor;
- reviewer for automation, operations and security;
- data-retention and export restrictions;
- written authorization and stop conditions for any active identity request.

## Prohibited practices

Do not infer personal email addresses, reuse procurement contacts for unrelated marketing, suggest that public project information reveals a vulnerability, or ask a non-technical executive to authorize production testing.
