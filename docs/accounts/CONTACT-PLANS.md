## Questions that reveal market ignorance

Do **not** ask:

- whether the company “has OT”;
- whether it already has “a mobile application”;
- whether it wants an “all-in-one scanner”;
- who “handles cybersecurity” without naming the site and workflow;
- which protocols exist across the whole enterprise;
- whether a phone may simply be plugged into production.

Those questions confuse product novelty with customer need. No direct phone-based OT assessment product was identified in the reviewed incumbent set, but customers already have adjacent tools, controls and suppliers. Ask instead:

1. Which existing task—walkdown, commissioning, maintenance preparation, audit evidence or inventory reconciliation—creates repeat work?
2. What is the current system of record: CMDB, CMMS/EAM, engineering database, OT-security platform or contractor dossier?
3. Who accepts the result operationally, who approves collection technically, and who owns the commercial framework?
4. Which collection methods are permitted on this exact environment?
5. What evidence and export format make an observation actionable?
6. Which incumbent integrator or auditor must participate?

## Contact-data rules

- Use only a company-published address, portal, switchboard or public professional profile.
- Never guess an email pattern or treat a personal address as a business lead.
- A profile supports a role claim; it does not prove reporting line, budget, contact consent or network authority.
- Record outreach date, route, response and title revalidation in the CRM.
- Stop after a clear refusal; do not automate high-volume employee outreach.
