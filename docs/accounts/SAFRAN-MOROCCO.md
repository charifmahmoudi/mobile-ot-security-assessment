# Safran Morocco account map

_Research baseline: 30 August 2026. Safran's Moroccan footprint is expanding rapidly; project companies and local leadership must be rechecked before engagement._

## Footprint and investment cycle

Safran reports more than 4,800 employees across ten Moroccan sites after 26 years in the country. The sites cover multiple businesses rather than one Morocco subsidiary: nacelles, wiring/electrical systems, aircraft equipment, engineering and engine services.

The new-project pipeline is unusually important:

- a €120 million engine-maintenance facility near Casablanca, planned for 2027;
- a €200 million LEAP-1A engine assembly line at MidParc, planned for 2028, intended to produce up to 350 engines annually and described as Safran's only such assembly line outside France;
- a €280 million landing-gear plant near Casablanca, planned for 2029.

This gives Atlas OT Scout a commissioning/handover opportunity before every site process and asset record becomes entrenched.

### Reconstructed project path

For a new facility: project director/industrial engineering → plant maintenance/industrial IT → group manufacturing IT and cybersecurity → HSE/quality → supplier management and procurement. For an existing site, local president/site director and maintenance replace the greenfield project organization.

The company has a visible professional network, but global aerospace security and intellectual-property rules make broad employee outreach inappropriate. The better routes are supplier events, MidParc project channels and official recruitment/procurement contacts.

## Technology and operating signals

Public sources found no reliable complete PLC/DCS/MES vendor inventory for Moroccan plants. Useful signals are narrower:

- local industrial-IT administration exists;
- industrialization roles mention automation and AI;
- new IT infrastructure/security recruitment was visible in August 2026;
- the new sites require maintenance, HSE, production, supply-chain and IT hiring;
- Safran's supplier priorities include aviation security, resilience, competitiveness and operational excellence.

The absence of a public vendor list must be preserved. A product roadmap should support common aerospace/manufacturing evidence sources but should not claim Safran Morocco uses Siemens, Rockwell or another stack without a job specification, partner case study or authorized observation.

## What external coverage says

Reuters emphasizes that the LEAP line will be Safran's only engine assembly site outside France and that the investments are meant to support high production rates and Morocco's aerospace supply chain. The significance is not merely plant count: these sites will carry global product-quality and cybersecurity requirements while being commissioned in a cost-sensitive local ecosystem.

Public recruitment volume and new-facility roles suggest rapid organization building. That can create an opening for standardized handover, but also means the correct buyer may not yet have a stable title.

## Opportunity designs

### Greenfield handover standard

Before line acceptance, define a vendor-neutral mobile evidence package for one utility or production cell: authorization, as-designed inventory import, passive observations, physical identifiers and signed exceptions.

### Approved supplier kit

A line builder uses Safran-controlled hardware/configuration and submits the evidence package with commissioning documentation. Safran retains encryption keys and final review.

### Existing-site utilities benchmark

At Safran Nacelles Morocco, start with a non-product utility/facilities network during planned maintenance rather than proprietary production processes.

## Product implications

- Strong IP/data controls, encrypted local storage and customer-held keys.
- Configurable disabling of camera, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and external export.
- Legal-entity/site/project hierarchy and supplier identity in every record.
- Handover comparison and reviewer sign-off rather than continuous monitoring claims.
- Export suitable for aerospace-quality and configuration-management records.
- Hardware and OS lifecycle evidence will matter for group approval.

## Unknowns

- Named directors for each new plant/project and the local industrial-cybersecurity owner.
- EPCs, line builders and automation suppliers.
- Safran global CMDB/manufacturing-IT standards applied in Morocco.
- Mobile-device/removable-media policy inside each entity.
- Supplier qualification and export-control restrictions.
- Whether MidParc/IMA can host a representative benchmark.
