# Safran Morocco

## Operating context

Safran reports more than 4,800 employees across ten Moroccan sites spanning engineering, wiring, nacelles, aircraft equipment and engine services. The footprint is expanding quickly: a €120 million engine-maintenance facility is planned for 2027, a €200 million LEAP-1A assembly line for 2028, and a €280 million landing-gear plant for 2029. Each is a new commissioning, supplier-handover and asset-governance opportunity.

This account cannot be treated as one factory. Safran Nacelles Morocco, Safran Aircraft Engine Services Morocco, Safran Electronics & Defense and other legal entities have local site leadership while group cybersecurity and aerospace-quality requirements sit above them.

## Public activity and events

Safran is a visible participant in Morocco's aerospace ecosystem. Aerospace Meetings Casablanca 2025 convened OEMs, suppliers and decision makers at MidParc, the location of planned Safran investments. Safran's supplier program emphasizes aviation security, resilience, operational excellence and on-time delivery. These themes support a proposition around controlled commissioning records and faster asset reconciliation, not a generic cyber pitch.

## Account entry thesis

The new MidParc facilities offer a better opening than an established production site: define a portable acceptance-test evidence workflow before the lines are fully operational. An approved line builder or industrial integrator could use the app under Safran's rules to capture interface and asset observations, then hand over a signed machine-readable inventory.

For existing sites, start in a training or utilities environment. Aerospace manufacturing has strict IP and supply-chain controls; local-first storage is useful only if encryption, key custody, audit logging and controlled export satisfy Safran policy.

## What must be checked

- Legal entity and project owner for each new plant.
- Group authority responsible for industrial cybersecurity and approved mobile tooling.
- EPCs, line builders and automation suppliers for the new facilities.
- Existing CMDB/MES/maintenance systems and handover formats.
- Supplier qualification, aviation-security, export-control and photography rules.
