# Account engagement playbook

## The commercial motion

The accounts in this repository already run plants, security programs, maintenance contracts and supplier frameworks. They do not need a stranger to tell them OT exists. The opening is a small, controlled improvement to a workflow they already perform: asset walkdown, commissioning handover, maintenance preparation, audit collection or inventory reconciliation.

### 1. Enter through a live operational moment

Use a disclosed expansion, shutdown, new line, digitalization project or audit cycle. The account dossiers identify examples: Stellantis capacity ramp-up, Safran's new MidParc plants, Managem's Tizert digitalization, OCP-MS industrial assessments and Cosumar's seasonal campaign preparation.

### 2. Bring the accountable triangle

A pilot needs three voices:

- the operational owner who knows what may be touched;
- the security/IT owner who defines collection and data rules;
- the existing auditor, integrator or maintenance supplier who can execute inside the approved commercial framework.

Executive sponsorship helps, but it does not replace these owners.

### 3. Ask for a benchmark, not product validation

Define a small environment with a known reference inventory. Compare the current method and the mobile workflow on:

- preparation and collection time;
- devices correctly reconciled;
- unsupported or ambiguous observations;
- number of manual transcription steps;
- reviewer time;
- safety-policy violations (target: zero).

Do not use “number of vulnerabilities found” as the first measure. It encourages intrusive behavior and does not establish workflow value.

### 4. Make the output fit the customer's system

Before building an integration, obtain a sample import/export format from the customer's CMDB, CMMS or asset tool. The prototype should produce a transparent JSON/CSV package and preserve packet/capture provenance. Connectors to commercial inventory tools come only after a real format is available.

## Public-contact discipline

Use company contact pages, procurement portals, conference introductions or public LinkedIn messaging. Mention the specific public activity that makes the approach relevant. Do not scrape employee networks, infer email formats or send high-volume sequences.

A good first message is short: identify the operational moment, state the constrained workflow, ask who owns it, and offer a one-cell/offline benchmark. Do not claim the company has a vulnerability or that public employee profiles reveal its architecture.

## Qualification gates

Advance an account only when the following are known:

1. legal entity and site;
2. workflow and current method;
3. operational and security owners;
4. approved execution/procurement route;
5. allowed interfaces and capture point;
6. data retention/export rules;
7. reference inventory and success measures.

If any of the first five is unknown, the opportunity is research—not pipeline.


## Questions that disqualify the seller

Never ask whether the customer has OT, whether it already has a mobile application, or whether it wants an all-in-one scanner. Product novelty is not evidence of demand. Established adjacent products already cover enterprise discovery through sensors, collectors and explorers: [Claroty](https://claroty.com/industrial-cybersecurity) describes multiple discovery methods across more than 450 industrial protocols, and [runZero](https://www.runzero.com/) covers IT/OT/IoT asset discovery. [Kali NetHunter](https://www.kali.org/docs/nethunter/) proves mobile penetration-testing capability, but it is not an OT-governed field-evidence workflow.

The diligence questions are:

- Which existing task creates repeat work: walkdown, commissioning, maintenance preparation, audit evidence or inventory reconciliation?
- Which CMDB, CMMS/EAM, engineering database or OT-security platform is the system of record?
- Which operational owner accepts the result, which security owner approves collection and which commercial entity buys?
- Which incumbent integrator, auditor or maintenance contractor must participate?
- Which capture points, active queries, radios, removable media, retention and export paths are permitted?
- Which measurable result justifies a change: fewer revisits, shorter acceptance time, fewer unresolved records or less reviewer effort?

The competitive claim is therefore narrow: a portable, offline-first evidence and reconciliation workflow may be an unfilled packaging gap. It is not proof that customers lack discovery capability, and it must be validated against their current process.
