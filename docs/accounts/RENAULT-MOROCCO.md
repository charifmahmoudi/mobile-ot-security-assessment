# Renault Group Morocco — account decision brief

_Public-source review: 30 August 2026. Titles and site responsibilities must be reconfirmed._

## Decision

**Pursue a supplier-handover or IFMIA training benchmark, not an enterprise discovery sale.** Renault publicly describes an audited cyber-defense program and deep in-house automation capability. The product must reduce field-documentation friction inside those controls.

## Why now

- Renault reports 394,465 vehicles produced in Morocco in 2025, nearly 10,000 employees, 82% exported to 63 destinations and 87 tier-1 suppliers ([Renault Group](https://www.renaultgroup.com/en/magazine/our-group-news/tangier-factory-the-industrial-and-logistics-hub-where-everything-comes-together/)).
- IFMIA has delivered 3.2 million training hours in automation, robotics and electrification; Renault says 30% supports the wider supplier ecosystem ([same source](https://www.renaultgroup.com/en/magazine/our-group-news/tangier-factory-the-industrial-and-logistics-hub-where-everything-comes-together/)).
- A regional IT leader publicly links Tangier operations to digital twins, operational data, AR maintenance and regular cyber audits ([CIOmove](https://www.ciomove.com/news/topics-theses/the-it-evolution-in-renaults-tangier-plant-a-grounded-approach/)).

## Entry route

1. Approach IFMIA or an approved line builder with a supplier-handover use case.
2. Compare a known cell inventory against passive capture on Renault-controlled hardware.
3. Report time saved, documentation exceptions and false identifications; Renault retains keys and approval.

## Stop/go questions

- Which group inventory/CMDB and OT-discovery tools are mandated?
- Who owns plant cyber approval in Tangier versus SOMACA?
- Are Android devices, cameras, radios or USB Ethernet allowed in production?
- Can a small vendor contract directly, or only through an approved integrator?
