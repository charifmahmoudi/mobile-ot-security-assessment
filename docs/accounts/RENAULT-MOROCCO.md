# Renault Group Morocco

## Operating context

Renault Group Morocco operates the Tangier plant and SOMACA in Casablanca. Renault reported 394,465 vehicles produced in Morocco in 2025, nearly 10,000 employees, and 82% of output exported to 63 destinations. Tangier alone is described as capable of up to 400,000 vehicles per year. A production network at this scale has strong uptime, traceability and group-standard requirements; an ungoverned scanner is a poor fit, while a supervised maintenance and inventory instrument may be useful.

The practical pilot group is more likely plant IT + automation/maintenance + cybersecurity than the country CEO. The executive is relevant for sponsorship only after a concrete operational case exists.

## What public activity says about the need

A CIOmove interview with Nadia Tazi describes Tangier's move from no independent internet connection to reliance on connectivity, and states that the plant has a comprehensive cyber-defense program that is regularly audited. This is one of the clearest public descriptions of the customer's current behavior: cybersecurity is not absent, so Atlas OT Scout must complement an established program and produce data that the existing program can accept.

Renault's Moroccan automation hiring names PLC, HMI/SCADA, Modbus, PROFIBUS and EtherNet/IP. Public staff profiles mention Siemens, ABB, KUKA and FANUC. These signals justify prioritizing safe recognition of those protocol families and device clues, but they do not establish the complete installed base.

## Ecosystem and events

Renault operates the IFMIA automotive training institute, which has delivered millions of hours of training in automation, robotics and electrification. That creates a safer prototype venue than a live line. Tanger Med and the surrounding supplier cluster are also part of Renault's logistics chain, so a successful tool may later extend to approved tier suppliers—but each company remains a separate authorization boundary.

## Account entry thesis

Propose a training-cell or maintenance-window exercise: collect passive Ethernet evidence and reconcile it against a known cell inventory. The result should be an auditable delta list, capture provenance and a review package—not a “risk score.” If Renault cannot approve a phone on a production network, the same software can first work on imported PCAPs and inventory exports.

Potential doors:

1. IFMIA or a Renault training/industrial engineering team for a controlled benchmark.
2. Tangier plant IT/automation with the regional CIO organization.
3. A Renault-approved integrator supporting automation maintenance.

## What must be checked

- Current Morocco plant IT and automation reporting lines.
- Group mobile-device and removable-media policy.
- Whether capture is permitted at a mirror port and how files may leave the plant.
- Current CMDB/industrial inventory platform and accepted import format.
- Whether global procurement must approve even a no-cost pilot.
